Research Overview

This work presents a distributed thermoelectric regulation framework designed for adaptive rooftop snow mitigation under cold climate conditions representative of northern Japan. The proposed system investigates how independently controlled thermoelectric nodes can cooperatively regulate rooftop temperature through localized thermal actuation, adaptive feedback control and spatial thermal diffusion.

Unlike conventional snow mitigation approaches based on resistive heating or manual removal, the proposed architecture seeks to provide localized thermal stabilization while minimizing unnecessary energy expenditure. Each thermoelectric node operates autonomously using embedded sensing, thermal estimation and adaptive control strategies while simultaneously interacting with neighboring nodes through passive thermal diffusion.

Distributed Thermoelectric Architecture

The rooftop surface is modeled as a two-dimensional thermoelectric grid composed of independently regulated thermal nodes. Every node includes a thermoelectric module, local temperature sensing, electrical monitoring and adaptive thermal regulation capabilities.

Thermal interaction between adjacent nodes enables distributed thermal balancing, allowing the system to compensate for localized cooling, environmental disturbances and partial node failures.

Thermoelectric Modeling

Thermoelectric performance is characterized using the classical figure of merit:

ZT = (S²σT) / k

where S denotes the Seebeck coefficient, σ represents electrical conductivity, T is the absolute temperature and k corresponds to thermal conductivity.

Voltage generation across each thermoelectric node follows:

Vi = α(Th,i − Tc,i)

where α is the Seebeck coefficient and Th,Tc represent the hot-side and cold-side temperatures respectively.

Thermal Dynamics

Local node dynamics are governed by transient thermal energy balance:

Ci(dTi/dt) = Qlocal − Qloss + Qneighbor

This formulation captures localized heating effects, environmental thermal losses and neighboring thermal contributions.

Thermal propagation throughout the rooftop is modeled through discrete thermal diffusion:

Tij(t+1) = Tij(t) + Δt(Qlocal − Qloss + k∇²T)

The resulting model enables simulation of thermal spreading, distributed stabilization and adaptive temperature balancing across the entire rooftop structure.

Adaptive Thermal Control

Thermal regulation is achieved using both conventional PID and adaptive PID control strategies. The adaptive controller dynamically adjusts its regulation gains according to the estimated thermal error, improving convergence speed while reducing oscillatory behavior and thermal overshoot.

u(t) = Kpe(t) + Ki∫e(t)dt + Kd(de/dt)

To improve robustness under noisy sensing conditions, a Kalman-based thermal estimation layer is incorporated into the regulation loop, providing smoother state estimation and enhanced control stability.

Simulation Framework

The simulation environment incorporates realistic environmental perturbations including thermal losses, snow accumulation effects, sensor uncertainty, distributed node interaction and fault scenarios. These conditions enable evaluation of controller performance under representative winter operating environments.

Key Findings

Simulation results indicate that adaptive PID regulation combined with Kalman thermal estimation achieves improved stabilization performance compared with conventional PID and ON/OFF thermal control approaches. The distributed architecture demonstrates reduced overshoot, improved convergence characteristics, increased robustness to measurement noise and successful operation under partial node failure conditions.

Future Directions

Ongoing research includes large-scale thermoelectric networks, digital-twin integration, AI-assisted predictive thermal regulation, distributed edge intelligence and future validation through physical rooftop prototype deployment.
